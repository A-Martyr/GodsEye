Privacy

GodsEye public demo · updated 4 November 2026

GodsEye reads number plates, so it is fair to ask what it does with you. On this demo: nothing.

The data you see

The city on this page is a simulation. The vehicles, their number plates and their journeys are generated by the software. No real person's plate, photograph or movement is shown, and this public copy refuses photo uploads, so none can be added.

What GodsEye records about you

  • No account, no cookies, no analytics, no trackers.
  • No request log. The web server's access log is switched off on this demo, and the plates you search are not written to the system's audit log.
  • One setting in your own browser: whether alert sounds are on, kept in local storage so the page remembers it. Clearing your site data removes it.

Other services this page uses

  • Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap, whose tile servers receive your IP address as any website's do. See the OpenStreetMap Foundation privacy policy.
  • Hosting is by Microsoft Azure App Service, whose servers receive your IP address as any website's do; the service's optional request logging is not switched on for this demo. See the Microsoft privacy statement.

In a real deployment

Reading plates on real roads is personal data, and GodsEye is designed around that: reads below a confidence floor are refused rather than stored, records expire under a retention rule, and every lookup of a plate is meant to be logged with who made it and why. Insights › Privacy in the control room shows those rules. A deployment would also need its own lawful basis and policy; this page covers only the demo.
